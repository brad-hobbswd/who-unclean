Chapter 10

Stop Calling Unclean What God Has Made Holy

The question in the title reaches its weight when we stop directing the question at someone else and allow the question to examine us. Who are you to call unclean what God has made holy? What authority lies behind our words, and how certain are we that our judgment reflects God's own declaration?

These chapters have moved from the pain of being labeled to the culture labels create, from inherited traditions to Peter's encounter, from spiritual authority to the experiences of people pushed away, and finally to the way Jesus met those others overlooked. The journey is meant to bring us to a choice about how we treat people.

Acts 10 describes God's inclusion of Gentiles and Peter's recognition that he must not regard people as common or unclean. The context matters. The passage does not answer every theological question by itself, yet Peter's words should make us cautious about attaching our own judgments to God's name without careful examination.

Study Scripture seriously. Hold convictions honestly. Remain accountable for the way those convictions are expressed. None of these responsibilities requires reducing a person to the question being debated. A human being standing before us deserves to be heard, not turned into an argument.

Someone reading these pages may have spent years wondering whether church is safe, whether prayer is welcome, or whether belonging requires hiding. They may need a patient listener more than another speech. Someone else may recognize words they once spoke and regret the harm caused. Responsibility begins with listening, acknowledging the hurt, apologizing without excuses, and allowing changed behavior to speak.

Belonging grows through ordinary choices: welcoming someone, protecting their privacy, refusing gossip, making room for questions, noticing who has been left alone, and remaining present when a conversation becomes uncomfortable. No one should have to disclose private details or become the subject of a debate to receive basic respect.

We may not have every answer. We can remain students of Scripture and of one another's experiences, willing for God to correct us. Humility does not make truth less important; humility reminds us that God is God and we are not.

Let the question remain as an invitation to examine our own conduct. Before closing a door or pronouncing judgment, ask whether our words reflect the mercy we have received. When the book is closed, the next chapter is written in the welcome we offer, the people we listen to, and the harm we are willing to repair.

May those who encounter us find honesty without cruelty, conviction without contempt, and compassion visible in the way we live.

And he said unto them, Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation; but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean.

Acts 10:28
