Chapter 4

Peter’s Vision: A Revelation Beyond Tradition

Peter's vision in Acts 10 begins during an ordinary time of prayer. He was not searching for controversy or trying to abandon the faith that shaped him. Yet God confronted him with a vision he could not easily fit into his existing understanding.

Peter saw a vessel descend from heaven containing animals he considered common or unclean. A voice told him to rise, kill, and eat. Peter objected, drawing on what he had always practiced. The instruction came again: what God had cleansed, Peter must not call common.

Peter did not immediately understand the vision. The account says he wondered what the experience meant. That detail is worth noticing. He did not pretend to have an explanation simply to appear certain. He reflected, and the events that followed began to clarify what he had seen.

Many of us hold beliefs that feel settled because they have been repeated, practiced, and reinforced. A challenge can feel unsettling even when our desire is to follow God sincerely. Peter's experience shows that sincerity does not make our understanding beyond examination.

While Peter was still considering the vision, messengers sent by Cornelius arrived. The Spirit directed Peter to go with them. Their explanation connected the vision to a real household and a real invitation. Peter welcomed the men and traveled with them the following day, accompanied by believers from Joppa.

The revelation was leading Peter beyond the rooftop. He could remain occupied with the meaning of the vision, or he could take the next step and meet the people connected to the message. He chose to go. His understanding would deepen through the encounter ahead.

Acts 10 concerns God's inclusion of Gentiles in the early church. The passage should be read within that setting, not used as a shortcut for every theological question. The challenge to Peter's assumptions, however, is unmistakable. God was preparing him to recognize a work he had not expected.

The messengers were waiting, and Peter was on his way. The next chapter takes us into Cornelius's home, where testimony, the message of Christ, and the work of the Spirit bring the meaning of the vision into clearer focus.

On the morrow, as they went on their journey, and drew nigh unto the city, Peter went up upon the housetop to pray about the sixth hour: And he became very hungry, and would have eaten: but while they made ready, he fell into a trance, And saw heaven opened, and a certain vessel descending upon him, as it had been a great sheet knit at the four corners, and let down to the earth: Wherein were all manner of fourfooted beasts of the earth, and wild beasts, and creeping things, and fowls of the air. And there came a voice to him, Rise, Peter; kill, and eat.

But Peter said, Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean. And the voice spake unto him again the second time, What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common.

This was done thrice: and the vessel was received up again into heaven.

Acts 10:9–16

Now while Peter doubted in himself what this vision which he had seen should mean, behold, men which were sent from Cornelius had made inquiry for Simon’s house, and stood before the gate, And called, and asked whether Simon, which was surnamed Peter, were lodged there. While Peter thought on the vision, the Spirit said unto him, Behold, three men seek thee.

Arise therefore, and get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them. Then Peter went down to the men which were sent unto him from Cornelius; and said, Behold, I am he whom ye seek: what is the cause wherefore ye are come?

And they said, Cornelius the centurion, a just man, and one that feareth God, and of good report among all the nation of the Jews, was warned from God by an holy angel to send for thee into his house, and to hear words of thee. Then called he them in, and lodged them.

And on the morrow Peter went away with them, and certain brethren from Joppa accompanied him.
