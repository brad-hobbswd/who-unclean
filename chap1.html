Chapter 1

Who Gave You That Label?

Some words stay with us long after the room has emptied. We may forget who was standing nearby or what happened next, yet remember the sentence that changed how we saw ourselves. A label spoken by someone believed to represent God can sound less like an opinion and more like a verdict: Am I welcome? Has God rejected me? Did I misunderstand everything I believed?

Such words may come from a pulpit, a family conversation, a prayer line, or a private meeting where someone finally found the courage to speak honestly. The speaker may move on quickly. The listener may spend years trying to reconcile those words with a faith once held close.

A label reduces a whole life to one description. Faith, kindness, work, friendships, responsibilities, hopes, and struggles disappear from view. Someone may continue attending church, singing, praying, and serving while wondering whether the congregation welcomes their presence or merely tolerates them.

Jesus' teaching about the speck and the beam asks us to examine ourselves before making another person the object of correction. The passage does not forbid discernment. It warns against approaching someone with a confidence that ignores our own need for mercy and clear sight.

Before we speak about another person's life, we should ask what shaped our judgment. Have we listened to their story? Are we responding to what we know, or to an assumption repeated until the assumption feels certain? Could our words have caused harm we never intended?

People who have been labeled do not all need the same response. Some need an apology. Some need time. Some want a conversation where they can finish a sentence without being interrupted. Others are trying to understand whether their relationship with God can survive what people said in His name. We should not prescribe healing for someone whose experience we have never heard.

Intent matters, yet impact matters too. Defending a belief does not excuse careless speech, public humiliation, or refusal to listen. Our conduct is part of our witness. Self-examination is not a retreat from conviction; it is a responsibility that comes before correction.

Who gave you that label, and what did you begin believing after hearing it? Perhaps someone else has carried a label you helped place upon them. The question invites us to take words seriously and to remember that no person is fully known through a single description. In the next chapter, we will look at what happens when labels move beyond one conversation and begin shaping the culture of an entire community.

Judge not, that ye be not judged. For with what judgment ye judge, ye shall be judged: and with what measure ye mete, it shall be measured to you again.

And why beholdest thou the mote that is in thy brother’s eye, but considerest not the beam that is in thine own eye? Or how wilt thou say to thy brother, Let me pull out the mote out of thine eye; and, behold, a beam is in thine own eye?

Thou hypocrite, first cast out the beam out of thine own eye; and then shalt thou see clearly to cast out the mote out of thy brother’s eye.

Matthew 7:1–5
