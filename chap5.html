Chapter 5

What God Has Cleansed

Peter's arrival at Cornelius's home brings the vision down from the rooftop and into a room full of people. The revelation is no longer something to ponder privately. Peter must respond to the household gathered before him.

He acknowledges that God has shown him not to call any person common or unclean. The words connect his understanding directly to the people in front of him. Peter is not simply reinterpreting a symbol; he is recognizing how the revelation changes the way he enters fellowship.

Cornelius explains why he sent for Peter and describes the direction he received. The household has gathered to hear what God has commanded. Their testimony is part of the moment. Peter has come to listen as well as speak.

Imagine the significance of Peter standing in that home. The encounter is not about Peter congratulating himself for being willing to cross a boundary. God had already been working in Cornelius's life. Peter's task was to recognize that work and respond faithfully.

Peter begins proclaiming Jesus: His ministry, death, resurrection, and the forgiveness offered through Him. He does not make the gathering about his former reluctance. The message directs attention toward Christ.

As Peter speaks, the Holy Spirit falls upon those listening. The believers who came with him are astonished. Peter recognizes what has happened and asks whether anyone can forbid baptism to those who have received the Spirit. He then directs that they be baptized.

The change in Peter is visible in what he does. He enters the home, listens, proclaims Christ, recognizes God's work, and responds. The encounter is not merely a revised opinion. His understanding changes the people he is willing to receive and the actions he takes.

For someone who has been judged before being heard, Cornelius's household may bring a painful question: what would happen if people listened to your story before deciding what your presence meant? People arrive with prayers, experiences, hopes, and questions. They are not simply waiting to be evaluated.

Acts 10 specifically describes the inclusion of Gentiles and God's work among them. Its context matters. The wider application asks the church to be cautious about assuming that human boundaries tell us everything about where God is working. Peter's testimony also shows that listening can be part of revelation. Cornelius's account was not a distraction; it helped Peter understand why he had been sent.

The story moves from revelation to relationship, from instruction to action. Peter did not remain outside discussing what might be happening. He entered, heard, witnessed, and responded. That brings us to the responsibility of those who guide others spiritually: how do we help people approach God without placing ourselves in control of the door?
