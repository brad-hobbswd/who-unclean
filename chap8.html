Chapter 8

Jesus and the People Others Rejected

Throughout the Gospels, Jesus repeatedly encountered people whom religious society had placed at a distance. Some were considered morally compromised. Others were viewed as ceremonially unclean, socially undesirable, or unworthy of association. Their circumstances often made them the subjects of conversation long before anyone bothered to understand their lives. Jesus consistently demonstrated a willingness to meet people where they were, and His interactions revealed a heart that reached beyond the boundaries others had established.

In Luke 19:1–10, Zacchaeus was a chief tax collector whose reputation preceded him. The people knew his occupation and had already formed their opinions. When Jesus approached him, the crowd did not celebrate the invitation. They murmured that Jesus had gone to be the guest of a sinner. Their criticism revealed how uncomfortable they were watching someone they considered undeserving receive attention from the Lord.

1 And Jesus entered and passed through Jericho.

2 And, behold, there was a man named Zacchaeus, which was the chief among the publicans, and he was rich.

3 And he sought to see Jesus who he was; and could not for the press, because he was little of stature.

4 And he ran before, and climbed up into a sycomore tree to see him: for he was to pass that way.

5 And when Jesus came to the place, he looked up, and saw him, and said unto him, Zacchaeus, make haste, and come down; for to day I must abide at thy house.

6 And he made haste, and came down, and received him joyfully.

7 And when they saw it, they all murmured, saying, That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner.

8 And Zacchaeus stood, and said unto the Lord; Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken any thing from any man by false accusation, I restore him fourfold.

9 And Jesus said unto him, This day is salvation come to this house, forsomuch as he also is a son of Abraham.

10 For the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.

Luke 19:1–10

Jesus did not wait for the crowd to approve His decision. He called Zacchaeus by name and announced that He would visit his home. That encounter changed Zacchaeus. His response demonstrated repentance, generosity, and a willingness to make things right. The crowd had seen a tax collector. Jesus saw a man who could respond to the presence of God.

The difference between those perspectives is significant. People often believe they know someone’s character through reputation, appearance, occupation, personal history, or a single circumstance. Such conclusions may prevent meaningful relationships from ever developing. Jesus demonstrated that a person should not be dismissed before an opportunity for genuine encounter has been given.

In Mark 1:40–45, a man with leprosy approached Jesus and asked whether He was willing to cleanse him. The man’s request revealed uncertainty about the willingness of someone with authority to respond compassionately. Jesus did not recoil from him. He touched him and declared that He was willing. The encounter demonstrated compassion through action, not merely through words.

40 And there came a leper to him, beseeching him, and kneeling down to him, and saying unto him, If thou wilt, thou canst make me clean.

41 And Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, and touched him, and saith unto him, I will; be thou clean.

42 And as soon as he had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him, and he was cleansed.

43 And he straitly charged him, and forthwith sent him away;

44 And saith unto him, See thou say nothing to any man: but go thy way, shew thyself to the priest, and offer for thy cleansing those things which Moses commanded, for a testimony unto them.

45 But he went out, and began to publish it much, and to blaze abroad the matter, insomuch that Jesus could no more openly enter into the city, but was without in desert places: and they came to him from every quarter.

Mark 1:40–45

The significance of that touch cannot be overlooked. The man had lived with circumstances that separated him from ordinary community life. Jesus responded in a manner that communicated care and dignity. His willingness to approach the man challenged the fear and distance surrounding those considered unclean.

Religious communities sometimes become so focused on avoiding what they consider undesirable that they forget the people standing outside their circles. Fear can shape the way someone approaches another person. Suspicion can replace curiosity. A person may become so concerned about maintaining appearances that genuine compassion becomes difficult to express.

Jesus never demonstrated that holiness required indifference toward human suffering. His ministry revealed a holiness that could enter difficult circumstances without losing its purpose. He spoke truth, called people toward God, and demonstrated compassion through His presence. The people who encountered Him were not treated as interruptions to His ministry.

Children also experienced the difference between Jesus and those who attempted to control access to Him. In Mark 10:13–16, people brought children to Jesus, yet the disciples rebuked them. The disciples apparently believed the children were an interruption or did not deserve the attention being given. Jesus responded by correcting the disciples and welcoming the children.

13 And they brought young children to him, that he should touch them: and his disciples rebuked those that brought them.

14 But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said unto them, Suffer the little children to come unto me, and forbid them not: for of such is the kingdom of God.

15 Verily I say unto you, Whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child, he shall not enter therein.

16 And he took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them.

Mark 10:13–16

His response challenged assumptions about importance. Those who appeared insignificant to the disciples were welcomed by Jesus. Their presence was not an inconvenience. The kingdom of God was not reserved for people who possessed status, influence, or a reputation that impressed others.

These encounters reveal something important about the way Jesus treated people. He did not allow the opinions of a crowd to determine whom He would approach. He did not permit religious expectations to prevent Him from showing compassion. He was willing to recognize the dignity of people whose circumstances had made them difficult for others to accept.

The church should carefully consider whether its treatment of people reflects the ministry of Jesus. Are we willing to speak with people whose experiences differ from ours? Do we approach unfamiliar individuals with curiosity or suspicion? Are we more concerned with what others might think of our associations than with whether someone needs kindness?

LGBTQIA+ individuals have often been placed in the position of watching the church from a distance while others discuss their lives. Some have been welcomed only under the condition that they remain silent about important parts of their experiences. Others have been excluded before a meaningful conversation ever occurred. Such experiences should cause believers to examine whether the way they approach people reflects the example of Christ.

The Gospel accounts do not provide permission to turn compassion into an excuse for dishonesty. Jesus spoke with purpose, and His ministry remained connected to the will of the Father. Yet the manner in which He treated people should never be separated from the message He preached. His words and actions worked together. Truth was not presented through humiliation, and holiness was not demonstrated through cruelty.

We cannot claim to follow Jesus while disregarding the people He consistently noticed. A congregation may possess impressive programs, carefully organized ministries, and strong biblical teaching, yet the character of its ministry is also revealed through the way ordinary people are treated when they enter the building.

The people others rejected were never invisible to Jesus. Their circumstances did not prevent Him from seeing them. Their reputations did not silence His compassion. Their presence did not threaten His authority.

Perhaps the church should spend less time deciding which people deserve attention and more time learning how Jesus gave attention to those others overlooked. His example remains a challenge to every believer who desires to represent Him faithfully. We cannot control every opinion surrounding us, but we can choose whether our conduct reflects the compassion, dignity, and mercy demonstrated throughout His ministry.
